Gemini - While both **peasants** and **farmers** are engaged in agriculture, the distinction between them comes down to **historical context, socioeconomic class, land ownership, and economic goals.** 

In short: **All peasants are farmers, but not all farmers are peasants.** 

Here is a detailed breakdown of the differences:

---

### 1. Economic Goal: Subsistence vs. Profit
* **Peasant:** Operates primarily on a **subsistence model**. They farm to feed themselves and their families. Any surplus food is typically given to a landlord as rent, paid as taxes to the state, or sold at local markets to buy basic necessities.
* **Farmer:** Operates on a **commercial model**. They view agriculture as a business enterprise. Their primary goal is to produce a surplus to sell on regional, national, or global markets for a profit.

### 2. Land Ownership and Autonomy
* **Peasant:** Historically (and in some modern developing contexts), peasants rarely owned the land they worked. They were tenants, serfs, or part of a communal land system, heavily beholden to a local landlord, noble, or government. They had very low social mobility and limited agency.
* **Farmer:** Farmers usually own their land or lease it through formal economic contracts. They operate as independent business owners, managers, or employees with full legal autonomy and property rights.

### 3. Historical Context and Era
* **Peasant:** Associated with **pre-industrial, feudal, and traditional societies** (e.g., Medieval Europe, Imperial China, Tsarist Russia). While peasant societies still exist in parts of the developing world today, the term is largely tied to history.
* **Farmer:** Associated with **capitalist, modern, and modernizing societies**. The term grew dominant after the Agricultural and Industrial Revolutions, which turned farming into an industry rather than a way of social survival.

### 4. Technology and Scale
* **Peasant:** Works on small, often fragmented plots of land using traditional, labor-intensive methods (e.g., hand tools, draft animals, rainwater irrigation). 
* **Farmer:** Works on varying scales (from small family farms to massive corporate agribusiesses). Uses modern technology, heavy machinery, synthetic fertilizers, automated irrigation, and data-driven techniques.

### 5. Cultural Meaning and Connotations
* **Peasant:** In modern English, "peasant" can carry a negative connotation, implying someone who is uneducated, backward, or of low social standing. In anthropology and sociology, however, it is a neutral term describing a specific rural class structure.
* **Farmer:** A neutral or positive term denoting a respected profession, business owner, or vital worker in society.

### A Modern Nuance: Smallholders
In the modern world, the line can sometimes blur. Agronomists often use the term **"smallholder farmer"** to describe people in developing nations who farm small plots of land largely for subsistence, but operate within modern market structures—bridging the gap between the traditional "peasant" and the modern "farmer."
